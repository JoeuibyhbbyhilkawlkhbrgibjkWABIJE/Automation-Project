SEMESTER 1 ONLY — 2026–2027
Students: Daniel and Thomas
Class length: 75 minutes per scheduled class
Project basis: Festo Automation Trainer Project Planning Document, Version 1.0
School calendar basis: Limestone District School Board, Ontario, 2026–2027

PURPOSE
This planner schedules the complete Festo Automation Trainer project for Semester 1 only. It preserves the submitted project's seven engineering phases, shared leadership model, deliverables, assessment structure, safety gates, and 84–125 hour overall project-duration range.

SEMESTER 1 SCOPE
Project work is scheduled from the beginning of Semester 1 in September 2026 until the January 2027 examination period. The target is to complete the project, final validation, demonstration evidence, documentation, and individual reflections before the exam period whenever possible.

No normal project development is scheduled for Semester 2.

CALENDAR CONSTRAINTS USED
- First day of classes: September 2, 2026.
- Semester 1 ends January 28, 2027.
- P.A. days/no student classes affecting Semester 1: September 1, October 26, November 20, January 29.
- Winter Break: December 21, 2026–January 1, 2027.
- Semester 1 secondary examinations: January 21–27, 2027.
- Major project work should be substantially complete before January 21.

75-MINUTE PERIOD MODEL
- 1 class period = 75 minutes = 1.25 hours.
- The source project's 84–125 hour range equals approximately 67–100 class periods.
- Planning target: approximately 80 productive class periods = 100 hours.
- This planner assumes the course meets often enough during Semester 1 to make approximately 80 project periods available. If the actual timetable provides fewer than about 80 usable class periods, the teacher should reduce scope or revise the target because the source project itself specifies 84–125 hours.

TYPICAL 75-MINUTE CLASS
- 5 minutes: attendance, safety check and task assignment.
- 50 minutes: primary hands-on/project work.
- 15 minutes: testing, documentation, saving/versioning work.
- 5 minutes: cleanup, shutdown and hand-off.

MERCY / CONTINGENCY POLICY
This planner includes recovery capacity for sick days, appointments, school interruptions, Thomas's inconsistent attendance, unexpected faults, failed tests and teacher-directed changes.
- A missed class is recorded as a missed or partial 75-minute project period.
- The first recovery option is the next designated mercy/recovery period.
- Do not rush or bypass electrical, pneumatic or safety verification to recover lost time.
- If one student is absent, the present student follows the coverage instructions instead of silently taking over all of the absent student's responsibilities.
- Repeated or extended absence should be discussed with the teacher.
- January 12–20 is protected as the final major recovery and contingency window before exams.

ASSESSMENT WEIGHTS
- Documentation & Investigation: 15%
- Pneumatic Analysis: 10%
- Electrical Interface Design: 20%
- PLC Design Documentation: 15%
- PLC Program Development: 20%
- Integration & Troubleshooting: 10%
- Final Demonstration & Reflection: 10%

SAFETY GATES
- Verify voltage compatibility before connecting interfaces.
- Use appropriate protection for inductive loads and solenoid drivers.
- Verify input isolation and safe start-up behavior.
- Do not proceed to the next engineering stage until the preceding stage is verified.
- An absence, appointment or deadline does not justify bypassing a safety check.

FINAL SUCCESS CRITERIA
The project is complete when:
- continuous operation is achieved;
- sensor reliability is verified;
- a completed assembly is produced correctly;
- fault recovery is demonstrated;
- documentation is complete; and
- the final demonstration/presentation is completed.
